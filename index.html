Your sheet splits the numbers into 40 groups of 5. Every group follows one rule: n, n+4, n+15, n+23, n+36. So group 1 is 1, 5, 16, 24, 37. Pick a game below, then choose a tab.

Top 5 predictions

In shortThese are the app's 5 best guesses for the next draw. They use the numbers that come up most, numbers that often come up together, and what a normal draw looks like. Our tests show these guesses don't reliably beat random picks, so treat them as an experiment.

    How the score works

    • Popular numbers (50%): numbers that have come up more often than average score higher.
    • Pairs (30%): numbers that have come out together before score higher.
    • Normal shape (20%): sets that look like a typical draw (total, odd/even and low/high mix) score higher.
    • No set has ever been drawn before, and no two sets share more than 2 numbers.

    Moon, stars or calendar?

    In shortWe test whether the moon or star signs change the numbers drawn. Made-up groupings "pass" the test just as often as the moon and stars do, so there's no real link.

    How the test works: we sort the draws into groups (for example by moon phase) and compare them. Then we shuffle the labels randomly 1,000 times. If the real groups differ more than almost all the shuffles, the difference is real.

    Beaten by = how many of 1,000 random shuffles did at least as well. Under 50 counts as a notable difference. The decoys can't mean anything, so watch how often they come out notable too.

    Formula lab

    In shortBuild your own prediction formula with the sliders, then press "Run the replay" to see if it would have worked on this year's draws.

    The formula gives every number a score, turns scores into chances, picks a set, and never repeats a past result. The steps below show the maths, with a plain-words version of each.

    The formula, step by step

    1. 1. How often it\'s drawn. fi = (ci + a) / (N + a·M), where ci is how often number i was drawn, N is all numbers drawn, M is the highest number and a is the prior. A large a pulls every number back towards fair when there's little data. In plain words: numbers drawn more often score higher, but we don\'t trust small counts too much.
    2. 2. How recently it came up. ri = e−gi/τ, where gi is the number of draws since i last appeared. A positive weight favours numbers seen recently, and a negative weight favours overdue ones. In plain words: numbers seen lately score higher, or lower if you prefer "overdue" numbers.
    3. 3. Your sheet. hi is the average recent hits of the groups on your sheet that contain i. In plain words: numbers in groups that have done well lately score higher.
    4. 4. Total score. Si = wf·z(fi) + wr·z(ri) + wh·z(hi), where z(·) rescales each part to mean 0 and standard deviation 1 so the weights compare fairly. In plain words: add the three parts together, using your slider weights.
    5. 5. Turn scores into chances. Pi = eSi/T / Σj eSj/T (a softmax). A low temperature T makes the formula stick to its top scores, and a high T makes it close to random. In plain words: higher scores get a bigger chance of being picked.
    6. 6. No repeats. The formula draws numbers from Pi without replacement. It rejects the set if every number in it appeared together in any past draw, or if the set shares more than the allowed amount with the last draw. In plain words: a set that has already been drawn is never used.
